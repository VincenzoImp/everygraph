Everygraph style · working name “Vernier”

A calm measuring instrument

Everygraph should feel like something you trust a number from: warm paper, soft black, one ink-blue accent, and hairlines instead of boxes.

Every number is exact, set in monospace, and says so when it has been cut short.

It is friendly through plain words and generous spacing, not through colour, rounded shapes or decoration.

The mark, the name, the colours

Two off-centre rings and a dot — a region, a sub-region, a node. Seven colours carry almost everything; the accent is the only one with a hue, and it means “interactive or selected”.

What is decided, what is still open

These pages apply the decisions on the left and show the open questions on the right as they currently stand, so they can be judged on real screens.

Decided

  • The system.The calm instrument: warm paper, soft black, one ink-blue accent, hairlines, almost no shadow.
  • The mark.The Nest — two off-centre rings and a dot — with the Seal for small sizes.
  • The typefaces.Inter for everything read; JetBrains Mono for numbers, code and identifiers.
  • Numbers in monospace.Exact, with thousands separators, tabular; rounded only in running text.

Still open

  • The rule for colouring the map.A few well-separated colours reused for regions that do not touch, or one colour per top-level region. See Data & map.
  • The default theme of the viewer.The specification proposes the dark canvas; both are drawn on the Viewer page.
  • The final data palette.Ten categorical colours are in the tokens; the first is the accent blue, which is one of the things to settle.
  • Raised by these pages.The small cut of the Seal, and a few token values that miss their contrast target. See Foundations, section 9.
