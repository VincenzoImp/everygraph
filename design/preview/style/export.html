Figures and things that leave the product

A map or a chart that leaves Everygraph has to stand without the viewer around it: in a paper, on a slide, in a blog post, in somebody's inbox. This page settles how those look, and the two file types people will see on their disks.

1. Exported map figure

The print style from the specification: white paper, black ink and the data colours, nothing else. No chrome, no accent. The caption and the scale of counts are inside the image and cannot be switched off; a map without them is easy to misread. Dashed outlines on this page mark the trim of each image and are not part of it.

The rules these figures follow

Without relying on colour

Colour separates neighbours and nothing more. Every distinction that carries meaning has a second carrier that survives a grey printer.

The key, enlarged 2×: every meaning and what carries it besides colour
MeaningCarried by
Which regionLetter, name, exact count
Found communitySolid outline
SubdivisionDashed outline
Many small communitiesHatched fill
SelectedDouble ring in black
Link strengthWidth; exact figure in the legend
A path and its directionDashed line, arrowheads, numbered points
A series in a chartLine style, marker shape, label at the line end

The dashed path with numbered points is a proposal: the specification only defines links for a selection. It is here because a path is the other thing people export.

Greyscale check: the 85 mm figure in colour and as a grey printer renders it, both enlarged 1.6×
Greyscale check with a selection and links (map only, 136 mm wide)

What survives: the kind of each region (solid, dashed, hatched), the selection (double ring), link strength (width), identity (letters and names). What does not: telling two unlabelled neighbours apart by hue, which is why colour never carries a meaning of its own.

2. Exported charts

Charts follow the same print rules. One measure per chart and one axis; a series keeps the colour its region has on the map and also gets its own line style and marker; the two or three numbers a reader will quote are printed exactly, the rest are read from the axis.

On screen these charts have a hover layer (a crosshair with exact values). An exported figure cannot, so it prints the extremes and ships the table.

How to read it

  • Nesting is exact. A region contains the addresses of the regions inside it; counts add up.
  • Area follows the number of addresses. Read the number when it matters.
  • Distance is not a measurement. Position keeps strongly connected regions close and has no other meaning.
  • No line does not mean no link. Links are drawn only for what you select.

    4. Embeds

    A map inside somebody else's article. The blog below is a neutral stand-in; the marked block is ours. The embed brings its own hairline frame, a title bar and the caption, and nothing else: no shadow, no banner. The dark canvas is the viewer's default and holds on a white page; the bar and the caption take the light theme here because the page around them is light.

    Where the transfers went in 2024

    Ada Moretti · 24 November 2026 · 6 min read

    Most of what moved last year moved inside a handful of groups. When every address that sent or received something is placed on one map, a dozen large communities take up less than half of it, and thousands of small ones fill the rest.

    The largest community, the one around exchange deposit addresses, holds 18.2 million addresses, about one in twelve. The next two are less than two thirds of its size.

    The embed keeps the caption even at 680 px. Below about 480 px wide the counts in the title bar drop first, then the caption folds to its first sentence with "more"; the frame and the "Open the full map" link never go.

    6. File icons

    Two files reach people's disks: .eg, a graph, and .egmap, a shareable map. Both are a sheet with the mark. The graph carries the mark on paper; the map carries it on a dark canvas with a caption line beneath, so the two differ in shape and tone, not in hue. Icon colours are fixed and do not follow the theme around them.

    In a file manager

    A neutral file manager, not ours. Ours are the two icons and the two kind names: "Everygraph graph" and "Everygraph map".
