1 · Mark

Two off-centre rings and a dot: a region, a sub-region, a node. The Nest is the mark; the Seal is the same drawing reversed out of a square, for small sizes and busy backgrounds.

Units of a 32-unit box; the grid shows one unit per square, heavier every 4. The inner ring sits 2.5 right and 2 down from the centre, the dot a further 1.5 and 1.2: the drift is always down and to the right. The stroke is the medium weight on purpose: thinner reads as fragile, heavier reads as a target.

SPEC.md fixes the Nest and the square's corner radius but not how large the Nest sits inside the Seal. Shown here at 87.5 % (a 2.6-unit margin to the square's edge); the dot of the Seal takes the accent of the opposite theme, because the square is an inverse surface. Both are proposals — see the open questions on the overview page.

3 · Colour

Warm paper, soft black and one ink blue. Every token in tokens.css is below with its value and the measured contrast of the text or mark that sits on it. The ratios are computed on this page from the live tokens (WCAG 2 relative luminance), not typed in.

Every token

Under each swatch: the pair that matters and its ratio. Text needs 4.5:1; large text, icons and control borders need 3:1; hairlines and fills that carry no information have no requirement and show the ratio only. A ratio below its requirement is printed in the error colour with a cross, and is listed again, in words, in section 9.

The accent next to the status colours

The accent must never be mistaken for a status. Each column is one colour as seen with typical vision, with protanopia and with deuteranopia (Machado et al. 2009, severity 1.0, applied in linear RGB). The number is the distance from the accent in OKLab, ×100; anything under 10 is flagged. Status colours are told apart from each other by their icon and word, not by hue; the line under each block gives the closest pair and shows why that rule exists.

How the map is built

Everygraph reads the file once, in order, and never holds all of it in memory. It first gives every address a number, then sorts the transfers by those numbers into 2,048 parts on disk. Communities are found on that sorted form, level by level, until the regions are small enough to draw. You can read the plan before it runs, and change the budget if the estimate does not suit the machine.

A region on the map is a community that was found, not drawn by hand. Its area is its number of addresses; its position only groups it with its neighbours. Distance on the map is not a measurement, and the caption under every view says so.

What a count means

Every number shown is exact unless it says “about”. When a result is cut short, it says so in place:

  • a table shows 20 of 1,312 rows and offers the rest;
  • a map draws the 200 strongest links and names how many it left out;
  • an estimate carries its unit and the word “about”.
  1. Open a file with everygraph ingest transfers.parquet.
  2. Read the plan. Press Enter to accept it.
  3. Open the map with everygraph map transfers.eg.
The budget is a hard limit. A step that cannot fit says so before it starts, with the smallest budget that would work.

A caption or footnote is set at 13/18 in the muted ink, and stays inside the same column.

7 · Motion

Three durations and one easing curve. Motion explains where something came from or went; it is never there to be noticed. Nothing bounces, overshoots or loops for attention.

The three durations

120 ms · --dur-fast · hover and press

The fill of a control changes; nothing moves.

190 ms · --dur-base · panels and fades

DeFi protocols22,870,455
A panel slides 16 px and fades in; map levels cross-fade.

320 ms · --dur-slow · flying across the map

The view travels to a region; the only motion long enough to follow.

The easing

--ease: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) — a quick start and a long, soft landing. The dashed line is linear, for comparison. The same curve is used for every transition, in both directions.

What never animates

  • Numbers. A count appears at its final value. No counting up, no rolling digits.
  • Results arriving. Table rows, list items and tiles appear in place; they do not slide or stagger in.
  • Text and layout. Nothing a person is reading moves while it is being read.
  • Status. A warning does not pulse, shake or flash. It is there, with its icon and its words.
  • Anything, with a bounce. No overshoot, no spring, no elastic settle.
  • Loading placeholders. Skeletons are still; the progress bar or spinner is the only thing that moves.
Reduced motion. When the system asks for less motion, every transition and animation is switched off (base.css does this globally): panels appear, the map cuts to its destination, and the spinner stops. A stopped spinner says nothing, so every loading state also carries words — “Reading… 1,270 of 2,048 parts”.
Two motions run longer than a token. The spinner (one turn in 800 ms, linear) and the indeterminate progress bar are continuous, so they have no place on the three-step scale. They are the only exceptions and have no token yet.

  • Every interactive element shows the same ring: 2 px, offset 2 px.

  • The map is reachable by keyboard: arrow keys move between regions, Enter opens one, Esc goes up a level.
