Who is best on what, in one line each:
- **Speed in RAM:** GVE on CPU, pLouvain/pLeiden on GPU. No paper read shows lj modularity ≥ 0.765 under 2 s or ≈ 0.75 under 0.76 s on a CPU; on our machine GVE gives lj Q 0.7223 in 1.02 s (FINDINGS idle baselines).
- **Modularity:** sequential Leiden iterated (Traag 2019, LiveJournal best of 10 replications × 10 iterations: Leiden 0.7739, Louvain 0.7653) and NetworKit's randomized PLM/ParallelLeiden; we are 0.6–0.7% below igraph/NetworKit at three iterations on lj (0.7647) and 1.2% below Traag's best-of-ten 0.7739.
- **Determinism at scale:** Mt-KaHyPar-SDet is the reference engineering paper (Louvain for partitioning, not Leiden). Grappolo without coloring claims "the same output regardless of the number of cores"; LD-Leiden is repeatable per thread count but its Q varies 0.38% on average across thread counts; pLeiden is synchronous and seeded but not claimed deterministic. Nobody publishes a Leiden with refinement that is bit-identical for any thread count, platform and memory budget.
- **Memory:** nobody publishes out-of-core modularity optimization at in-RAM quality; CluStRE is the best streaming method and sits 4–7 points below Louvain on social graphs.
- **Hierarchy for maps:** no paper evaluates a bounded-fan-out community hierarchy at 10^9 edges; the closest working systems are ASK-GraphView (2006, ≤ 16 M edges) and graspologic's size-capped recursive Leiden.

## 6. What to ignore, and why

- **Graph HAC (TeraHAC, ParHAC/nearly-linear HAC, SCC):** built for similarity (k-NN) graphs and binary dendrograms; distributed, or "12–24 hours" for 2–3 B edges on one machine by the authors' own estimate.
- **Nested SBM as the production algorithm:** about 10^7 edges on a laptop (Peixoto 2021, i9-9980HK); graph-tool is C++/Boost, against D1. Worth keeping only as an offline evaluator on small graphs.
- **Infomap, RelaxMap, GossipMap:** another objective; Infomap and RelaxMap ran out of 128 GB on Friendster and uk-2007-05 in the DSLM study; hierarchical Infomap's largest published network is 5.5 M links.
- **SCoDA:** Q 0.0334 on Friendster as CluStRE ran it from disk; GPL-3.0.
- **Weighted sketches (Sahu 2024):** save per-thread table memory that we do not spend (earlier survey).
- **LD-Leiden, DF-Leiden, incremental Leiden:** dynamic graphs; not deterministic; out of scope for v0.1.
- **Distributed Louvain (Vite, Que et al., DSLM, ParLeiden):** distributed by design; only their synchronous-round and coloring lessons transfer (used above).
- **Exact modularity (Bayan):** graphs up to about 3,000 edges; useful only to calibrate the oracle on tiny graphs.
- **Paris as the main algorithm:** sequential Python, ≤ 3.2 M edges in the paper, binary dendrogram; at most a fallback to split one indivisible leaf (challenge 4).
- **Dasgupta's cost as the map metric:** read at abstract level only; defined for binary similarity hierarchies; not a validated measure of map readability.
- **GPU Leiden (pLeiden, cuGraph, gLeiden):** a GPU is never required (DESIGN § 7); read for its refinement idea only.
