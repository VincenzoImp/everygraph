Second wave, area **approx-rust**. Part A covers topic 5 of `docs/research/sota-coverage.md`
§ (c), approximation and sketching under a memory budget. Part B covers topic 9, Rust
parallel practice.

- `docs/research/memory-bounded-algorithms.md` § F, which recommended HyperBall, RK and KADABRA-later;
- `docs/research/sota-kernels.md`, which covers Rusty-PBBS, counter-based RNG and the branded indices.

Two pieces of work in development are touched. **The edge-map worktree (#44, branch
`m3-01-edge-map`, uncommitted)** is affected in § 3 B and challenges 5–7. **The `.eg`
container spec (PR #170)** needs no change: § 3 A explains why.

## 2. The state of the art, as a short map

### A. Approximation

| Task | Approach | Who is best | Numbers (their machine) | Printable guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neighbourhood function, harmonic/closeness/Lin for **all nodes** | HyperBall: one HLL counter per node, union = register max, one scan per hop | Boldi–Vigna (4, 5); Rust port: `webgraph-algo` + `card-est-array` | Wikipedia 4.2 M nodes / 101 M arcs, 2^12 registers, 100 runs: the error on the centralities is "very close to the theoretical prediction for each single HyperLogLog counter (1.62%), and almost always significantly smaller". At the minimum precision, 16 bytes per node. ### B. Rust parallel practice

| Question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Is Rust + Rayon competitive with C++? | **RPB vs PBBS/OpenCilk** (1): 14 benchmarks on an AWS c5.metal, 24 cores on one socket, rustc 1.69, Rayon 1.7. Rust is gmean **1.09× faster at 1 thread and 1.44× slower at 24**, with `unsafe` used for the irregular patterns. "RPB scales worse than PBBS on all benchmarks", which the authors attribute partly to "Rayon's runtime management". |
| Where does safe Rust stop being zero-cost? | (1) classifies the accesses in parallel code (Table 3, Fig. 3). 29% of them are irregular: 9% arbitrary writes (AW), 13% single indirect writes (SngInd), 7% ranged indirect writes (RngInd). The fear levels: <br>• **fearless**: read-only, Stride (`par_iter_mut`), Block (`par_chunks_mut`), divide and conquer; <br>• **comfortable**: RngInd, through their `par_ind_chunks_mut` with a monotone-offset check whose overhead is "negligible"; <br>• **comfortable but slow**: SngInd with a uniqueness check, 2.8× on lrs; <br>• **scared**: AW. <br>Replacing `unsafe` with checks or synchronization costs 1.3× and 2.1× on average at 1 and 24 threads. **Relaxed atomic loads and stores without RMW cost "negligible"**; a Mutex where no atomic exists costs 4× (hist). |
| What do run-time checks cost? | (2): 22 programs from The Algorithms plus 10 from the Benchmarks Game, on an i7-8700 with rustc 1.61. Rust vs C: 1.96× with every check **including integer-overflow checks, which release builds disable**; 1.77× without those; 1.27× also without bounds checks. Bounds checks are 52.7% of the overhead. These are tiny programs (~50 lines), so the figure is weak evidence for kernels. Ours: removing bounds checks took WCC from 1.36× to 1.03× and BFS from 1.16× to 0.99× of the lab loop (orkut, 1 thread, exploratory on a loaded machine; #44 worktree, `edge_map/node.rs`). |
| Safe unchecked indexing | Branded lifetimes (Beingessner 2015). GhostCell (13) **proves the unchecked-indexing API sound in RustBelt**. Our `Node<'g>` uses the same invariant-lifetime brand. |
| SIMD | `std::simd` is still nightly-only, tracking issue 86656 (21). Since 1.86, a safe function may carry `#[target_feature]`. Since 1.87, most `std::arch` intrinsics without pointer arguments are safe to call inside such a function (20). **Loads and stores stay `unsafe`**; `safe_unaligned_simd` wraps them (21). `pulp` and `fearless_simd` give safe dispatch, wasm included (21). |

## 3. Decision by decision

### A. Approximation (m3-09, D14, "What 'repeatable' promises")

| Our position | Best known work | Verdict | Evidence and consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| m3-09: HyperBall for distances, closeness, harmonic; "bit-identical for any thread count, chunking or regime" | HyperBall (4) | **Level on the algorithm, but the claim is under-specified** | Max-union is commutative and idempotent (11 lists it as HLL's "reproducibility"), so the order of unions does not matter. Bit identity still needs four rules m3-09 does not state, and they belong in m3-09 and m3-11: <br>(i) **Jacobi double buffer**: counters of iteration t are read while those of t+1 are written. In-place updates mix radii and make the distance sums depend on the schedule. Boldi–Vigna write ⟨v, a⟩ to disk and apply after the scan. <br>(ii) A **seeded, fixed hash** of the node id, with a "run r" seed derived from (seed, r). <br>(iii) **An estimator without libm**. The classic HLL small-range estimator uses `ln` (linear counting), which is not correctly rounded across glibc, macOS and wasm. Ertl's improved raw estimator (12) uses only +, ×, √ and the constant 1/(2 ln 2), and can be table-driven over C₀/m. <br>(iv) **Termination by stabilization**, never a fixed hop count: "any alternative termination condition may lead to arbitrarily large mistakes" (4). Per-node f64 harmonic sums accumulate in t order, so they are deterministic. |
| m3-09: "Every approximate result states its sample size and error bound" | (4) prints an empirical sd over runs; (6) a CV bound; (7–9) (ε, δ) | **Behind: we cannot print a proven bound for HyperBall harmonic/closeness** | Print what is true: the RSD per counter (1.04/√m, or ULL's) for *reachable-set sizes*; for harmonic/closeness, "no proven bound; empirical sd over R runs = …" when R ≥ 2. A proven per-node CV needs ADS+HIP (6), at 30–60× the memory (3). |
| HyperBall vs ADS/HIP/Oasis under a budget (coverage question) | (3) never compares with HyperBall | **HyperBall stays the default; ADS only as a future index** | Arithmetic, an estimate. HyperBall's Friendster state at m = 64 HLL registers: 48 B per counter × 2 arrays × 65.6 M nodes ≈ 6.3 GB, plus 1.05 GB of f64 accumulators. At m = 16: ≈ 1.6 GB. ADS at Oasis's soc-LJ density (8.0 GB ÷ 4.8 M ≈ 1.7 KB per node at k = 16) would be **≈ 110 GB for Friendster** (extrapolation; ADS grows with ln n). At equal per-node error (1.04/√m = 1/√(2(k−1)) ⇒ m ≈ 2.2k), ADS costs about 30–60× more bytes. ADS wins only for post-hoc node weights β, pairwise distance and closeness-similarity queries, and many repeated queries (an index). Not a v0.1 product feature. |
| HyperBall when n·m registers exceed the budget | Oasis's partitioned construction: destination partitions × source partitions, memory chosen by the user (3) | **Gap in m3-09/m3-03** | HyperBall reads c[w] at random for neighbours, so its counter array is O(n·m) "state" (D11). Oasis's scheme applies to HyperBall unchanged: keep one destination block of counters in RAM, stream the source blocks' counters and the in-edges of that block. Max-union makes the result independent of the partition count. **Budget changes only time**, as the promise requires. |
| m3-09: "betweenness sampling (RK, seeded, fixed sample count) batched with multi-source bitset BFS" | KADABRA's bb-BFS path sampler + SILVAN or Bavarian progressive bounds (7–9) | **Behind: the batching idea does not fit betweenness** | MS-BFS batches *reachability* with 64 sources per word. Brandes' source sampling also needs σ and δ per (source, node), about 20 B per node per source: ≈ 1.3 GB per concurrent source on Friendster, so a 64-wide batch ≈ 84 GB (arithmetic). Path sampling (RK/KADABRA/SILVAN) needs only the nodes touched by one bb-BFS (about m^½ edges per sample, 7) and **integer** hit counts. Integer counts are order-free, hence bit-identical for any thread count and budget, if each sample's RNG is keyed by (seed, sample index) (counter-based, kernels report challenge 10) and stopping is tested only at the fixed points of a geometric schedule (8, 9). KADABRA needs random access (both directions on directed graphs, D6), so it uses the m3-01 random-access path. Keep MS-BFS for **exact sampled closeness/harmonic pivots** (16): distance sums are popcount × level, integers again. |
| m3-09 acceptance: "top-100 overlap ≥ 90% with exact" | KADABRA and SILVAN have a **top-k mode with guarantees** (7, 8) | **Adjust** | An additive-ε bound says nothing about the order of a flat tail. Use the top-k mode for `top()`, and print "indistinguishable from 0" for nodes with b̃ < ε. Exact betweenness is infeasible on lj/orkut. Validate on com-dblp, com-youtube and amazon (SNAP, exact Brandes feasible): max observed error ≤ printed ε, and the overlap. |
| m3-09: "orkut approximate betweenness in < 60 s" | No orkut row in 7–9 | **Still unsupported** | Our papers report seconds to minutes on graphs up to wikipedia-link-en, on 48–64 cores. Keep the target as "to be set by the first measurement", as the issue already says. |
| m3-09: "KADABRA-style adaptive stopping only at fixed batch boundaries" | SILVAN/Bavarian: a geometric schedule m_i = m₀θ^i is part of the bound's union-bound term (8, 9) | **Level; make it the method** | Schedule points are data-defined. KADABRA's own check frequency is heuristic; adopt SILVAN's fixed schedule. Memory: SILVAN's c = 25 Rademacher sums, dense as i32 on Friendster, ≈ 6.6 GB (arithmetic). Store them sparsely (only nodes on sampled paths) or reduce c (Bavarian Fig. 1a: returns diminish fast in k). |
| "Labelled approximate variants" (DECISIONS, D14) | Nobody states it | **Ahead in intent, missing the rules** | Two rules. (1) Precision parameters (m, k, ε, δ, sample schedule) are **never derived from the budget or the thread count**: api-sketch § 2.8's cache key excludes `memory` and `threads`, so a budget-derived m would make two budgets return different bits under one key. (2) Approximate results are marked like truncated ones (api-sketch § 2.5): `.approximate`, `meta["approximate"]` = {method, seed, samples, guarantee kind, ε, δ or RSD, runs}, the same keys in the provenance record's parameters. **No change to the `.eg` spec (PR #170)**: provenance already has free key/value parameters and unknown kinds can be added later. |
| Triangle/degree sketches | Wedge sampling (17), TRIÈST (18) | **Not needed** | Exact degrees are free, and exact triangles on Friendster take ~70 s at 16 threads (sota-kernels). Wedge sampling is the only cheap labelled variant worth a line, for `transitivity(approx=…)`: integer counts, seeded by wedge index, a Hoeffding bound to print. |
| Sparsification for budgeted kernels | (10) | **Reject for kernels; consider for the viewer** | No bound on the downstream answer, and it is a different graph, against "never substitutes a lower-precision variant". For deep-zoom edges (m5-07), Local Degree (keep the top neighbours of each node by degree, ties by id) is deterministic and keeps hubs and connectivity (10). |

| Our position | Best known work | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|

| #44 WCC/BFS: `AtomicU32` with `Relaxed`, CAS only when hooking | (1) § 7.4: relaxed loads and stores without RMW are "almost zero-cost"; priority update (sota-kernels) | **Level** | A relaxed RMW is still `lock`-prefixed on x86; keep the read-check-then-CAS shape. Never put per-node state that a later scan reads in bulk into atomics: LLVM does not vectorize atomic loads (inference). |
| **Ranged indirect writes** (CSR build m2-08, coarse graph m4-03, members per community in m4-01): no facade helper today | (1) `par_ind_chunks_mut`: split by monotone offsets, a cheap check, "comfortable" | **Behind: a gap that invites `unsafe`** | The lab Leiden does exactly the patterns (1) calls scary. `lab/b2-leiden/src/main.rs` writes through `SyncPtr` for Block patterns that `for_each_chunk_mut` already covers. It scatters `members` with `AtomicUsize::fetch_add` positions (SngInd), which also gives a **member order that depends on the schedule** unless sorted later. And it writes coarse targets by offset ranges (RngInd). Safe alternatives: Block → existing facade. RngInd → a new `par::for_each_range_mut(data, offsets, f)` (check monotone, split with `split_at_mut`, no `unsafe`). SngInd scatter → a parallel sort of (key, id) pairs: deterministic because keys are distinct, safe, O(n log n). Or a two-pass counting sort over fixed blocks where few buckets exist. |
| D13 "no gain past 16 threads" for memory-bound kernels | (1): Rayon scaled worse than OpenCilk on every benchmark at 24 cores, "Rayon's runtime management is also worse" | **Open question** | Part of our flat curve may be runtime, not bandwidth. Our tasks are coarse (≥ 64K entries), which should hide it (inference). Cheap A/B in challenge 6. |
| `everygraph-format/src/adjacency/simd.rs`: `unsafe` on all six `Lanes` primitives plus dispatch | Rust ≥ 1.87 (our MSRV is 1.87): safe intrinsics inside `#[target_feature]` functions; only loads, stores and the dispatch call stay `unsafe` | **Slightly behind; low value** | The `Lanes` trait forces `unsafe fn`, because a trait method cannot carry `#[target_feature]` unless it is unsafe (my understanding of the target-feature-1.1 rules; verify before acting). Per-arch free functions would cut the `unsafe` to loads, stores and dispatch. `safe_unaligned_simd` would remove the loads and stores too, at the price of a dependency (pure Rust, so D1 holds). `std::simd` remains nightly, so not an option. `pulp`/`fearless_simd` add a dependency to save ~100 lines that Miri and the three ISA tests already cover. |
| Rayon determinism | (1) does not address it | **Ahead** | The facade fixes chunk boundaries by grain, returns results in chunk order, and has no thread-count getter. Rayon is not a dependency of the kernel crates, so its order-dependent float `sum`/`reduce`/`fold` cannot be called there. The #44 drivers reduce over fixed node blocks, never over tasks. |

## 5. Our novelty claims, checked

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Sampled betweenness that is bit-identical for any thread count and any memory budget | **Not found.** KADABRA, SILVAN and Bavarian parallelize "with minimal synchronization" and make no determinism claim (7, 8, 9). It follows from known pieces (integer counts, counter-based RNG, fixed schedules), so it is a modest novelty |
| HyperBall results independent of threads, chunking and regime | **Not new as a property.** "Reproducibility … by a commutative insert and a commutative and associative merge" is listed for HLL by Ertl (11). The rules of § 3 A (Jacobi, no libm, stabilization) are what we must add |
| The answer does not depend on the budget, only the time (for sketches) | **Implicit in Oasis** (3): the partition count changes memory and time, and the ADS is defined by an equation independent of it. Oasis does not claim bit identity. Our explicit promise is new in wording, not in substance |
| Branded node indices for unchecked kernel indexing (#44) | **Technique not new** (Beingessner 2015; proved sound in 13). Using it in a graph framework, with lists validated by the source: **not found** (also the kernels author's verdict) |
| A safe facade that makes Rayon results thread-count-independent | **Not found** as a published API. (1) covers safety, not determinism. Fixed reduction blocks themselves are not new (sota-kernels § 5) |

## 6. What to ignore, and why

- **ADS/Oasis as v0.1 kernels**: 30–60× the graph in sketch space (3); HyperBall answers the same all-nodes questions at about 1/30–1/60 of the bytes (arithmetic). Revisit only for pairwise distance or similarity queries.
- **Sparsification as an approximate kernel**: no error bound on the answer (10); breaks "never substitute silently".
- **TRIÈST, DOULION, count-min degree sketches**: we store the graph; exact triangles and degrees are cheap.
- **ABRA**: superseded by Bavarian and SILVAN (8, 9).
- **Bavarian's `bp` (source-sampling) estimator**: worst ε per second (9) and O(n) floats per in-flight source.
- **`std::simd`**: nightly-only (21).
- **`pulp` / `fearless_simd` for the codec**: a dependency to replace ~100 lines that the three ISA backends and Miri already cover.
- **ASE'22's 1.77× as evidence for our kernels**: tiny programs, overflow checks on (2).
- **Mutex-based synchronization in kernels**: 4× in (1)'s hist; atomics or ownership splits always exist for our state.
- **KADABRA's heuristic check frequency, and NetworKit's thread-local stopping** (not read): use fixed schedule points instead.
