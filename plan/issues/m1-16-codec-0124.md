**Question.** In our codec every gap costs at least one byte plus two control bits (10 bits), including the many gaps of 1 that BV codes as intervals. That floor is why the byte tier stays at 11.4 bits/entry on uk-2005 against BV's 2.1 (FINDINGS § C, shared machine). Lemire's Stream VByte library ships a 0124 variant (word lengths 0, 1, 2, 4 bytes). In it a consecutive neighbour costs 2 bits, and 3-byte words grow to 4. Does a block codec 2 that the encoder picks per block when smaller pay off, and on which graphs?

**What the literature says** (`docs/research/sota-storage.md` § 4, challenge 2). Nobody has measured the variant on graphs. The gain depends on the share of gaps equal to 1: large on web graphs and after a locality ordering; zero or negative on unordered social graphs.

**Method**
- [ ] Offline first, no timing: a histogram of word byte-lengths per block on lj, orkut, uk-2005 and web-google, before and after the best order of {{#m1-09-hierarchy-order}}. From it, the exact bits of codec 0 and codec 2 per block, and the total with per-block choice. The original order can be counted now; the reordered half waits for that experiment's permutations
- [ ] Only if the offline count shows a gain: decode speed as an interleaved A/B in one process (single thread and 16 threads), codec 0 against codec 2, on the graphs where it wins
- [ ] Cost written down: a second shuffle table, the decoders (scalar, SSSE3, NEON, `simd128`) kept identical, the fuzz target; a new codec byte value, which version 1 readers already refuse (`docs/spec/eg-adjacency.md` § 4, § 6)

**Done when.** A table graph × order × {bits/entry codec 0, codec 2, per-block choice}, decode speed where measured, and a go/no-go for codec value 2 in {{#m2-03-adjacency-codec}}'s format.

**Feeds.** {{#m2-20-archival-codec}}, which waits for this and for {{#m1-09-hierarchy-order}}.

**Less `unsafe` when the codec changes next (low priority; added 2026-11-25, `docs/research/README.md` R7).** `crates/everygraph-format/src/adjacency/simd.rs` marks all six `Lanes` primitives and the dispatch `unsafe`. Since Rust 1.87 (our MSRV) most `std::arch` intrinsics without pointer arguments are safe inside a `#[target_feature]` function; only loads, stores and the dispatch call stay `unsafe`, and the `Lanes` trait forces `unsafe fn` only because a trait method cannot carry `#[target_feature]` (the report's understanding of the target-feature 1.1 rules; verify before acting). If this experiment leads to a codec change, the new decoders use per-architecture free functions; not worth doing on its own (`docs/research/sota-approx-rust.md` § 3 B, challenge 10).

**Pointers.** `docs/research/sota-storage.md` § 3 D4 and m2-20, § 4 challenges 2 and 6; Lemire, Kurz, Rupp, Stream VByte (IPL 2018) and the `lemire/streamvbyte` README (`streamvbyte_decode_0124`).
