**Why.** This is the largest open technical risk (DESIGN § 5.1). Today Leiden on Friendster needs 22.6 GiB of anonymous memory; the only run that fit 16 GiB was Louvain with a poor partition (Q 0.4647; not reproduced by the tracer, which got Q 0.6038 but a hierarchy the map cannot use, {{#m1-03-louvain-collapse}}). Friendster Leiden, one iteration, 16 threads, idle: 82 s; anonymous peak 37.0 GiB raw / 34.3 compressed with the graph in heap — compression alone does not bring it under 16 GiB (#118). The tracer ({{#m1-01-e2e-tracer}}, 2026-11-25, idle, `bench/results/tracer/20261125T122609Z-3e764d115d86.jsonl` rows 9–15) located the failure: under 16 GiB both Leiden and the fan-out-capped Louvain are killed in pass-0 aggregation after the local moves and refinement complete, thrashing on the mapped graph (1.80 TB and 2.72–2.74 TB read); with hierarchy output the capped Leiden is killed even at 56 GiB (55.8 GiB anonymous, `lab/m1-01-tracer/results/logs/communities-leiden-56G.warmup1.measure.json`) because every level's quotient graph is kept in memory (about 51.5 GiB by arithmetic; not tested without hierarchy output).

**Measured by {{#m1-02-ooc-aggregation}} (2026-11-25, idle; PR #143; FINDINGS § M1-02; `bench/results/ooc-leiden/20261125T160906Z-e501e2cf5727.jsonl` and `…20261125T174959Z-b61036e59740.jsonl`; Friendster, async Leiden, 1 iteration, 16 threads, f64 weights, level 0 in the D4 compressed store).** The lab design works and is what this issue productionizes (`lab/m1-02-ooc-leiden`):
- *Sequential aggregation:* one streaming pass in node order writes (c(u), c(v), w) into range-partitioned buckets, each held in RAM while the accountant allows and written to a file otherwise, then sorted, combined, encoded and committed in bucket order (`src/seqagg.rs`). Out of core 20.2–26.8 GB of buckets go to files at 16–4 GiB (`bucket_disk_bytes`), and level-0 aggregation costs 1.62× its in-RAM time at 16 GiB (26.3 s against 16.2 s) and 1.94–2.01× at 8–4 GiB.
- *Spilled levels:* every quotient graph is written as a weighted compressed store and memory-mapped; with hierarchy output the level graphs stay on disk, never in memory.
- *Accountant reservations:* every large allocation is reserved first (`src/acct.rs`); the anonymous peak stayed under the hard limit in all 31 rows. The review of #143 found items that were not reserved, and the production accountant must cover them: the next level's label array (`next_bound`, 71.5 MB at Friendster level 1, more than the 64 MiB slack), the split-community assembly (`z` and the encoded bytes), the scatter positions (`pos`), output growth beyond the estimate (the reservation must grow, not only shrink), the per-thread `tl`/`wl`/`tmp` buffers of the final phase, and the quotient graph's file-backed record for its whole lifetime. They were covered by over-reservation, not by design; the lab now reserves them (`--check-agg` remains unaccounted). Production should also own and resize per-level buffers instead of `dontneed` behind live borrows.
- *Abort on a violated invariant:* a row-sum, weight-overflow, combine or self-loop violation ends the run at once with a status (the lab's first version hung until the harness timeout, review item S1); test `lab/m1-02-ooc-leiden/tests/fault.rs`.
- *ENOSPC is a refusal, not a crash:* every spill write and create fails with a status (exit 5 in the lab). There is still no disk budget or free-space check; the planner needs one (buckets up to 26.8 GB on Friendster), and an interrupted hierarchy run must not leave its directory behind.
- *Skew:* a giant community is split over several buckets by a hash of the target, which spreads distinct targets, not records; a giant whose members mostly link to one community can make the run refuse at a budget an even spread would fit. Split by measured record counts.
- *Exactness assumes integer weights:* identity across budgets rests on every weight sum being an f64 or u64 sum of integers below 2^53 (exact in any order) and on u32 weights. Weighted inputs break both the bit-identical strengths and the identity across budgets; they need a fixed summation order (or fixed-point weights) in aggregation, and a test of identity across budgets on a weighted graph.
- *Measured against the targets below, restated against the same algorithm in RAM (74.8 s, nothing spilled; the target ratios are unchanged):* 16 GiB 87.4 s, **1.17×**; 8 GiB 175.3 s, **2.34×**; 5 GiB 2.88×; 4 GiB 3.49×. 0 disconnected everywhere. Async Q median 0.6014 at 16 GiB with one repetition at 0.5981 (spread 0.598–0.607 in RAM and out of core); deterministic under 16 GiB: hash `2a12e5882874c38d`, the in-RAM one, Q 0.6055. Map-shaped under 16 GiB: 164.1 s, Q 0.5900. orkut under 447 MiB (50% of its edge array): 3.10× the same algorithm in RAM (6.01 s against 1.94 s) — above this issue's 3× target. Below 16 GiB the cost is level-0 local moving re-reading the store 6.0×, 8.7× and 12.8× per sweep at 8, 5 and 4 GiB in block-permuted order.
- *Visiting-order candidates tested:* windowed block permutation (L2) loses block order's quality unless the window covers the graph; the bitmap sweep (L3) finds nothing to skip at level 0; not materializing level 1 (L4) is rejected on arithmetic; bumped hashing (L5) not tested; the seeded relabelling with storage-order visits (L1) kept Friendster at 1.28× and 1.43× under 8 and 4 GiB and is under study in {{#m1-14-relabel-at-ingest}}. ADR 0015 is unchanged.
- *Not measured:* more than one iteration, the deterministic mode below 16 GiB, other graph families at this scale, thread counts other than 16.

**Scope.** Productionize the outcome of {{#m1-02-ooc-aggregation}}:
- [ ] Level 0 over the streaming source with O(n) state reserved through the accountant
- [ ] Sequential bucket aggregation; level-1 graph written to disk and processed through the same streaming source
- [ ] Planner integration: per-level regime choice, preflight estimate of state + level-1 size (22–44% of the input entries in the lab)
- [ ] Visiting order: block-permuted order reads the edge array in runs of 64 consecutive nodes; raise the block to ~1k nodes for streamed levels (no quality loss measured up to 1,024) and schedule blocks to follow disk order within a pass
- [ ] Each level's quotient graph written to disk as it is produced and dropped from memory ({{#m4-06-hierarchy-artifact}}); memory does not grow with the number of levels (the tracer's capped Leiden with every level kept: killed at 56 GiB, `bench/results/tracer/20261125T122609Z-3e764d115d86.jsonl` row 10 and its warm-up log)
- [ ] Included in the no-OOM matrix ({{#m3-10-no-oom-ci}})
- [ ] The visiting order out of core is a fixed function of the graph and the seed, never of the budget (otherwise "same result for any budget" breaks). Candidates from the survey (`docs/research/memory-bounded-algorithms.md`), to be tested in {{#m1-02-ooc-aggregation}}: decorrelation by a seeded relabelling at ingest with storage-order visits; a windowed block permutation of fixed width; the active set as a bitmap swept in disk order with block skipping; level-1 never materialized (stream level 0 through community[], Graphyti); bumped two-phase hashing in aggregation (TeraPart). If one changes ADR 0015's order, the ADR is amended
- [ ] Kept self-loops of the input go on the quotient graph's diagonal (`docs/spec/graph-semantics.md` § 5.6); the lab's sequential aggregation treats a self-loop in its input as an invariant violation (its inputs never had one) — the production aggregation must accept them and count them as the spec says, with a test against the oracle (#147)

**Acceptance**
- Friendster under `MemoryMax=16G`: completes, Q ≥ 0.60, 0 disconnected, ≤ 2.5× the in-RAM time
- The same with the map hierarchy output on (fan-out-bounded levels for {{#m4-07-map-hierarchy}}): completes under `MemoryMax=16G`, with bytes read reported (the tracer's map-shaped runs were killed in pass-0 aggregation after 1.80–2.74 TB of reads, `bench/results/tracer/20261125T122609Z-3e764d115d86.jsonl` rows 9, 11–12)
- orkut with a limit of 50% of its edge array: ≤ 3× the in-RAM time (lab: 5.6× at 100%, OOM below)
- Deterministic mode works out of core with the same hash as in RAM
