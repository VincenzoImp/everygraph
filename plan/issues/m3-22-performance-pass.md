**Why.** Under the rule "official runs only when a number is published" (CLAUDE.md § 6, owner 2026-11-26), issues close on correctness and exploratory evidence, and their missed speed targets come here, those of M2 issues included (M2 has no performance pass of its own). All numbers below are exploratory (interleaved A/B on a loaded machine) unless a result file is named.

**Gaps to close**
- [ ] Build (#35): lj 1.30–1.84 s vs ≤ 0.5 s, orkut 5.7–6.2 s vs ≤ 1.0 s (PR #215). Profile on orkut: parsing twice 26 %, finishing buckets 18 %, dictionary collection 13 %, shared atomic item counts 11 %, id look-ups 10 %, scatter 12 %. Friendster targets not yet measured.
  - [ ] The build's column items, with an interleaved A/B each (PR #269 review; item size 30 bytes in memory after its fix, 28 + a 2-byte local): carry only the declared columns (weight only: 4 + 8 + 8 = 20, plus 2); drop `at` under `"sum"`, whose aggregates (exact sum, count, min, max) do not depend on order (weight only: 4 + 8, plus 2 = 14); keep the column values in memory when the build is in RAM (today every value goes through the `Stored` scratch files, 8 bytes written and read again). `fill_lists` reads the out columns in the count pass too, which does not need them.
- [ ] Edge-map per-task overhead (#44): WCC at 16 threads 1.13× the lab (official, `bench/results/edge-map/20261126T043042Z-c0d7f248bb47.jsonl`): reader slot and scratch handed out once per pass, not per task (PR #224's analysis).
- [ ] BFS (#49): 1.33× GAP on orkut; SSSP 2.06× GAP on orkut, 2.4× on roadnet-ca (PR #220): `NodeSet::list_into` per step (20 %), rayon overhead (14 %), cost-check pass (10 %).
  - [ ] BFS with parents takes about 4.3× the time of BFS without them (orkut, raw CSR in the heap, 16 threads, from the hub, 4 interleaved rounds; exploratory, `lab/m3-12-reference-runs/results/ab-bfs-parents-orkut.txt`, PR #250). The deterministic parent (the smallest node one hop closer, `docs/api-sketch.md` § 2.12) costs a second pass over each reached node's whole list, with an atomic minimum per hit; GAP and GBBS keep the first parent their traversal writes. Candidates: find the parents during the push (S6 below), or a cheaper deterministic rule. The official BFS rows time BFS with parents, so this cost is in every one of them.
- [ ] k-core (#50): 1.4–1.9× the lab at 1 and 16 threads after the per-round fixes (PR #218): per-entry bucket check and Philox draw per hub edge are the candidates.
  - [ ] The exact counts of sampled hubs: on twitter-sym they read 3.53 G entries, 1.47× the graph and 59.4% of all k-core entries, in 1,173 calls (counts, `lab/m3-27-frontier-counters/README.md`, PR #278). After S3, 47% of k-core's cycles on twitter-sym are in them (perf, exploratory, PR #291). Further k-core gains need fewer exact counts, not a better driver.
- [ ] **S3, the frontier driver** (`docs/research/sota-review-2026-11.md` S3; {{#m3-27-frontier-counters}} recommends it for k-core sub-rounds and SSSP relaxations): runs cut by entries from a degree prefix sum, hub lists split for commuting push bodies. Success: k-core on twitter-sym ≤ 0.7× of today, no case slower than 1.05×; abandon below a 10% gain. **Built by PR #291:** k-core on twitter-sym 0.777 [0.699, 0.853] of main, every other case within 1.05× (ratio of medians with 95% interval, interleaved, raw CSR in the heap, 16 threads, exploratory, run `20261126T204703Z-a30859103d65`). Short of 0.7×; the rest is the exact counts above.
- [ ] Compressed BFS/WCC: whole-list decode (decode surplus 8.5–20.6× for BFS, 1.9–2.75× for WCC; {{#m3-27-frontier-counters}}): prefix decode with early exit for BFS (S10), and for WCC decode only the records of the nodes that pass the filter and draw the sample from a short prefix. **Built by PR #291:** BFS decodes 1.47× (orkut), 1.86× (twitter-sym) and 1.87× (Friendster) the entries it examines; WCC decodes 44 M, 435 M and 594 M entries against 234 M, 2,462 M and 5,133 M (counters, compressed store mapped, 16 threads; hashes identical). What is left feeds {{#m1-17-chunked-hub-records}}'s go/no-go.
- [ ] **S6, out-of-core BFS: direction chosen by bytes, parents found during the push** (`docs/research/sota-review-2026-11.md` S6). Success: bytes read ≤ 0.6× of today with identical result hashes, on Friendster at 8 GiB from a cold cache, then on europe-osm.
- [ ] **S7, SSSP: one lazy frontier, local bins, pull before push** (Δ*-stepping in PASGAL, GAP's bins; `docs/research/sota-review-2026-11.md` S7), after S3. {{#m3-27-frontier-counters}} found the driver is not the cause on lj and orkut: the gap is the passes per step and the cost representation (f64 column against GAP's int32 pairs). Success: ≤ 1.2× GAP on lj and orkut; abandon if not below 1.3×. One run with integer costs separates the representation from the algorithm.
- [ ] PageRank (#47) on raw CSR: the official edge-map run of 2026-11-27 has `pr32` on orkut at 16 threads 1.20× the lab (`bench/results/edge-map/20261127T000435Z-2510b9c16c0a.jsonl`). PR #NNN cut the strength pass, the loop scan and the exact sums per block; after it, `pr32` is 1.085× and `pr64` 1.081× the framework's first PageRank (`proof::pagerank` at c0d7f248) at 16 threads, 1.050× and 1.017× at 1 thread (exploratory, interleaved). What is left: the pass that counts the loops of an undirected graph for its strengths (one read of the lists), the f64 strengths read in every iteration, and the inner loop's code (3.5 instructions per entry against 3.0).
- [ ] PageRank (#47): weighted on a per-pair column within 15% of the unweighted kernel on orkut; the out-of-core cost against in RAM from interleaved rounds ({{#m3-19-harness-interleave}}), not from a fixed run order.
- [ ] WCC sampling (#48): first-neighbour against hybrid k-out sampling, interleaved, on orkut, Friendster and a web graph in clustered order (uk-2005, once in the dataset registry).
- [ ] BFS under a memory limit below the adjacency size (#49): Friendster in at most the time of three sequential reads of the graph file.
- [ ] WCC (Afforest) on community orders: 1.2–2.0× slower than on input ids (`lab/m1-09-hierarchy-order/results/README.md`); cause not yet measured.
- [ ] In-adjacency transpose (#38, PR #239): twitter-2010 at 16 threads 1.28–1.50× the lab's raw-array transpose (11.7–38 s at load 18–43, target ≤ 15 s); the candidates are the count pass (random atomic increments) and the scatter. A cap on items per bucket (2^22 tried in PR #239) is a candidate for build rounds held up by one bucket of hubs; it needs an interleaved A/B on two graphs.
- [ ] Strong components (#223, PR #277): BGSS without VGC (local search per task) and hash bags. Exploratory, interleaved, Tarjan time over BGSS time, raw CSR in the heap: at 16 threads web-google 1.69, roadNet-CA 1.20, soc-Epinions1 0.42 (Tarjan faster); at 1 thread 0.17, 0.43 and 0.37. On roadNet-CA the first round's two BFS take about 0.8–1.0 of the BGSS time (exploratory, interleaved). Work: BGSS reads 1.75 m entries in all on twitter-2010 and 4.2 m on web-google, Tarjan m.
- [ ] Planner (#45, PR #249): its choice within 10% of the best manually chosen configuration for PageRank, WCC and BFS on orkut, twitter-sym and Friendster at budgets of 2, 8, 16 GiB and unlimited; first the official `everygraph-bench run edge-map --official --only planner-` that replaces the cost model's exploratory constants
  - [ ] The fallback to fewer threads compares no speed: it takes the largest smaller thread count that streams, so a budget just above the state can turn 16 memory-mapped threads into 1 streamed thread (PR #254's final check). Choose by `cost::seconds`, or at least say it in the plan document.
- [ ] Block cache (#178): Leiden level-0 local moving on Friendster under 8 GiB within 1.5× of in RAM (ADR 0029), and fewer bytes than per-phase `MADV_RANDOM` at equal memory, before D11 lets the cache replace the advice on local files. Exploratory (`bench/results/block-cache/exploratory/20261126-friendster-review.jsonl`), deterministic, one level, 16 threads, cold, resident peak 7.1 GiB both: the cache read 304.7 GB (K = 0) and 330.7 GB (K = 64), the map with `MADV_RANDOM` 262.6 GB; the cache took 1.44× in RAM at K = 64 (load 22 against 16), the map 2.9× (load 29). Fast mode 1.2–1.8× (sweeps vary from 4 to 6). Hypotheses for the bytes are in ADR 0029, "The cache as built"; the deterministic mode reads a frame again in many sub-rounds, refinement most.
- [ ] Same-machine comparison with GAP/GBBS/NetworKit/igraph/GVE (#55's reference runs) decides which gaps matter most.

**Done when.** Each gap is either closed (interleaved A/B against the reference, ratio stated) or its remaining cause is written down with a follow-up; the milestone-end official run records the final numbers.
